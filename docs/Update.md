**~ Post-match battle report with plan reveal** — *on hold, built but hidden*

- The game's whole conceit is hidden simultaneous orders, but players never see what the opponent actually planned — replaying both committed plans round by round turns the core mechanic into a payoff and teaches the game better than a tutorial.
- A static per-round summary shipped and was judged the weaker half of the idea; it should be a **replay**, not a table. The UI is hidden (`report-reveal` carries `hidden`) and nothing on the match-end path feeds it. Server-side recording in `BattleReport.cs` + `GameLoop` is kept and still tested, so the data any replay needs is already there: both crews' committed orders, ability targets, dodges, deaths, and hill state per round.
- **Blocked on determinism.** The match cannot be re-simulated: movement steps on `Time.deltaTime` inside `yield return null` loops (`Movement.cs:195`), `Time.timeScale` scales those steps, bullets are PhysX rigidbodies resolved by collision callbacks, shot cadence quantizes to frames via `WaitForSeconds`, target ties break on `FindGameObjectsWithTag` order (`Shooting.cs:447`), and `UnityEngine.Random` is a global stream shared with VFX. Seeding the spread alone changes nothing.
- Two ways forward: (a) record combat events — shooter, target, damage, timestamp — and render them on a timeline beside the plans, cheap and needs no determinism; or (b) convert combat/movement to a fixed-timestep seeded simulation, which is a rewrite but is also the prerequisite for the seeded self-play the experiments catalog wants (EXP-A02/A03).

- [x] **Unit prefabs: base plate and body alignment** — fixed in the prefabs, not worked around in the menu.
  - Gameplay seats a unit by its **capsule collider bottom** (`Helper.heightOffset`), so that is the board plane. Measured against it, only the Sniper was built right: the Commander, PogoRider, and Soldier floated their base puck 0.23 above the board, the Shotgunner floated its puck 0.60, and four of the five had feet sunk through or hovering over their own plate (−0.28 to +0.12).

  - All five now put the puck bottom exactly on the collider bottom and the feet exactly on the puck's top face, so the puck is the lowest visible thing on every unit. Colliders were deliberately left untouched — they are the hit and line-of-sight volume, and `heightOffset` assumes they stay centred on the root.

Aligning to that padding seats the padding on the ground and leaves the unit hovering by exactly that much. Both the prefab pass and `CharacterCarousel.TryFindLowestPoint` read true vertex positions instead, baking skinned meshes first.
  - This was a gameplay defect, not only a menu one: feet were clipping into the board and the Shotgunner's cell marker hovered. All 270 edit-mode tests pass after the change.

Models therefore stand in their authored pose. Acceptable for now against the toy-shelf read, but the roster wants a shared idle.

- [x] **Guided first match** — shipped as a sandboxed tutorial, reachable any time from the title's Tutorial button
  - Built as a **sandbox rather than prompts layered over a bot match**, which was the earlier plan. In a bot match you hope the teaching moment arises; in a sandbox you place the pieces so it must. The durability argument still holds because the script sets starting positions and opponent intent, never outcomes, so a cooldown retune or a sixth character can't invalidate it.
  - One continuous 1v1 Elimination match, fog off, no visible lesson breaks. Both crews field a Soldier because Grenade is the only ability that reaches across the teaching distance *and* opens a dodge window on its target, so one unit covers the ability lesson and the dodge lesson from both sides. Column 6 is the only wall-free column on the board, so neither crew can leave the lane.
  - Three lessons, all keyed to what the player has actually done rather than to a round number, so ignoring a prompt stalls that lesson instead of desyncing the script: draw a route and lock in (payoff: the unit stops and opens fire by itself), throw the Grenade (payoff: the opponent steps clear, teaching the telegraph rule from the caster's side), then answer the opponent's own throw. Closing lesson names the unit card, the one gesture a lone unit never needs.
  - Copy is split across **two surfaces**: imperative instructions ("Lock in.") stay on the small line above the dock, while the three explanatory lessons get a larger card anchored above it. The card sits over the dock rather than the screen centre so a lesson never lands on top of the fight it is describing.
  - **Nothing in the sandbox runs on a clock.** Planning was already untimed with the countdown hidden; lesson cards now carry a dismiss button and persist across phase changes until the player presses it, and the dodge window is opened to the same length as planning, closing the instant a dive is drawn and released. That last one matters most: the shipped window is `timeDivePerUnit × alerted` = three seconds, which is no time at all to read a first prompt and answer it. Dodge responses have no lock-in chip, so the commit hangs off the drag release instead.
  - **The sandbox cannot kill.** `Health.TakeDamage` floors tutorial damage at 1 HP, so hits still land and still read on the health bar but neither crew can be eliminated. Found the hard way: a student who fumbles the dodge dies exactly where a first-timer is most likely to fumble, which ended the match in a defeat before the closing lesson ever ran. This also removes a whole class of script fragility, since the enemy dying early would have cut the lesson short too.
  - **Ends on the last lesson, not on a wipe.** Dismissing the closing card calls `GameLoop.FinishTutorial`, which closes the match with a new `MatchResultReason.TutorialComplete` reading "Tutorial complete." from either seat instead of claiming a win. The open planning session is ended first so the board stops taking orders behind the results overlay.
  - The join scene is only a NetworkManager bootstrap for the tutorial, so its setup chrome is hidden on the way through and the player sees the screen's own backdrop rather than a flash of the match-setup lobby. Arriving there with a host already requested means the boot failed, which drops the session and restores the lobby instead of stranding the player on an empty screen.
  - **Play again replays the tutorial** rather than dropping the student into a crew-selection screen they have never been shown. The host is still up at that point, so `TryStartReplayIfReady` reconfigures the sandbox and reloads the board directly instead of routing back through the lobby.
  - **The scripted opponent dives toward the student**, so the round it throws back opens from a range that actually threatens them instead of from wherever "away from the blast" happened to land it. Searched breadth-first over the cells a dive can reach rather than as four straight runs, because the cell that both clears the blast and closes the gap is usually round a corner — stepping straight at the student is blocked by the student. Escape is measured as Euclidean distance against `abilityRadius`, since the blast resolves on a radius and a diagonal neighbour is still inside it however many steps it took to get there.
  - **Nudged toward the enemy** by withholding the lock-in prompt: a route that ends farther from the opponent gets "Move toward the enemy." instead. Nothing is blocked, and the ability lesson applies the same nudge when the student has wandered out of throwing reach, so the script is recoverable rather than dead-ended.
  - **Crew size was not made variable.** Rosters stay the configured length so `RosterRules.Validate` and `ConfigureTeam` are untouched; only the number of units spawned changes, via `GameLoop.UnitsPerTeamThisMatch`. `SetFieldedCardCountClientRpc` trims both card strips to match, which also keeps ordinary matches honest.
  - The opponent is scripted through the existing planning seam: `TutorialDirector.CreateEnemyPlan` overwrites `submittedTeamPaths` for the opponent each round. `BotPlayer` is deliberately left constructed so dodge windows still get a real, tested dive without any scripting.
  - Two runtime traps this surfaced, both now handled: `currentPhase` flips to `"dodging"` at the top of `RunDodgePhase` *before* the server works out who is threatened, and a window every alerted team answers at once (the scripted opponent always does) opens and closes inside a single frame without ever surfacing to `Update`. `GameLoop.WasTeamAlertedToDodgeThisRound` is the round-scoped record that replaced polling `dodgeAlerted`.
  - Covered by 7 edit-mode tests pinning the fragile assumptions — catalog index, dodge-capable ability, spawn separation against live weapon and ability ranges, a wall-free lane, the completion result copy — plus `DevTutorialE2ETest`, a self-stopping harness that plays the whole tutorial from the title screen, dives out of the incoming blast, dismisses each card, and asserts every line appeared on the beat and surface it was meant to — including that the closing card sits untouched for eight seconds rather than timing out and that Play again redeploys the one-unit board from the top. 277 edit-mode tests and 50 runtime checks pass.
- [x] **More maps** — five boards, selectable in the lobby. Design and measurements in `docs/design/MapPlan.md`.
  - The shipped board is named **Concourse** and is unchanged: same 18 walls, same hill, same spawns. This is a pure addition, so nothing about how the game already plays moved.
  - **A board is now data, not code.** `MapDefinition` is `walls` + `hill` + `spawns`; `MapCatalog` holds them. `GameLoop.wallLayout` and `KingOfTheHillCells` became properties over the active entry, keeping their old names and shapes so grid line-of-sight, path validation, the bot, and the arena builder were all untouched. **Adding a board is one catalog entry** — the lobby list, the character-select preview, the builder's checks, and the acceptance tests all enumerate the catalog.
  - **The map rides the existing replicated `MatchOptions`.** This is the part that had to be right rather than convenient: clients validate paths and compute fog against `wallLayout` locally, so a peer on the wrong board would silently reject legal moves. Routing every write through `MatchOptions.SetCurrent` means host and client cannot disagree, and an unknown id from a different build degrades to Concourse instead of a null layout.
  - Layouts are authored as **ASCII pictures**, the same ones in the plan doc, so a drift between design and code is visible at a glance instead of buried in 34 `Vector2Int` constructors.
  - The four new entries span one dial — **how exposed the objective is**, measured as off-hill cells with line of sight to an average hill cell. Concourse 48, Foundry 20, Causeway 18.5, Bastion 11. That range is the point: it lets KOTH's failure modes be read by comparison rather than argued from a single board. **Concourse Oblique** varies deployment instead of geometry (corner-weighted spawns on Concourse's own blockout), which takes spawn-to-hill from a flat `3,2,2,2,3` rounds to `3,3,2,1,1`.
  - **The plan's acceptance gate is now a test.** `MapCatalogEditModeTests` runs point symmetry, single-region connectivity, hill and spawn clearance, slot-for-slot mirrored deployment, hill reachability within four rounds, and unique/resolvable ids against every entry. It caught real defects while the layouts were being drawn — disconnected regions on two candidates, spawns sitting inside walls on a third.
  - **The map preview was a lie waiting to happen.** The character-select thumbnail is a baked PNG of Concourse, and would have shown Concourse's cover whatever board you picked. The drawing moved out of the editor-only generator into a runtime `MapPreviewImage`; the screen now renders the chosen board and the editor still bakes Concourse, so the freshness smoke test passes byte-for-byte.
  - One test had swapped `GameLoop.wallLayout` directly to isolate ability-path geometry, which the property made impossible. Rather than special-case it, `MapDefinition.Scratch` gives tests and tools an explicit off-catalog board.
  - **Not playtested.** 283/283 edit-mode tests pass and every measured figure was re-derived inside the editor against the real `GameLoop`, but no new board has been played — in Play mode, against a bot, or in PvP. Bot behaviour is the specific risk: it sends every KOTH unit at the hill, which Oblique's round-one hill access may reward far too well.
